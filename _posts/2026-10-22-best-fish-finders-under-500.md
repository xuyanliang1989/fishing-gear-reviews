## What to look for

- **Sonar type and power.** Traditional 2D sonar is the baseline. CHIRP (compressed high-intensity radar pulse) gives you cleaner target separation than old fixed-frequency units. Down imaging and side imaging are the upgrades that actually change how you fish structure — but they cost screen real estate and money.
- **Screen size and brightness.** A 5-inch screen is fine on a kayak or a tiller boat; a 7-inch is the sweet spot for most bass boats. Look for a unit rated for direct sunlight — a dim screen is useless at noon.
- **GPS and mapping.** Internal GPS with a decent basemap (or the ability to add a chip) is worth paying for. Waypoints, trails, and contour maps turn a depth finder into a navigation tool.
- **Transducer included.** Some "deals" are head units only. Confirm the transducer is in the box, and check whether it's transom-mount, trolling-motor mount, or both.

## FAQ

**Do I really need side imaging on a fish finder under $500?**
Not necessarily. Side imaging is great for covering water and finding structure, but it eats screen space and adds cost. If you fish mostly vertical presentations over known structure, traditional CHIRP and down imaging will serve you fine and leave more budget for a bigger screen.

**Is a 5-inch screen too small for a bass boat?**
It depends on how you fish. A 5-inch unit is fine if you're running a single sonar view, but once you split the screen between sonar and GPS, things get cramped. If you have the console space, a 7-inch unit is worth the extra money.

**Do these units come with a transducer?**
Most bundles do, but not all. Always check the listing — some "head unit only" deals look cheap until you add the transducer. The units in this roundup are typically sold with a transducer included, but verify before you buy.

**Can I use a fish finder without GPS mapping?**
Yes, and plenty of anglers do. GPS is most valuable for marking waypoints and navigating unfamiliar water. If you fish the same handful of spots, traditional sonar alone is perfectly adequate and saves money.

**Will a budget fish finder work on a kayak?**
Absolutely — in fact, kayaks are where 5-inch units shine. Look for a unit with a compact transducer and low power draw, and consider a portable battery box. The Lowrance HOOK Reveal 5 and Humminbird Helix 5 are both popular kayak choices.
