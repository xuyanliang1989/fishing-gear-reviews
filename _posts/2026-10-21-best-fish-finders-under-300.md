## What to look for

- **Screen size and brightness over resolution.** A 5-inch screen you can read in direct sun beats a 7-inch screen that washes out at noon. Look for at least 480 x 800 pixels and a unit anglers report as sunlight-visible; transflective displays hold up best in glare.
- **Sonar type.** Standard 2D CHIRP is the baseline and covers most freshwater fishing. Down imaging helps you read structure and separate fish from cover. Side imaging is the big upgrade, but at this price it usually means a smaller screen or a bundle with a transom transducer.
- **GPS and mapping.** Built-in GPS with a decent basemap is worth more than extra pixels for most anglers. Check whether the unit takes a mapping card (Navionics, C-MAP, LakeMaster) and whether the card is included or extra.
- **Transducer and mounting.** Confirm the unit ships with a transom-mount transducer and that the cable length and mounting hardware match your boat. A cheap head unit with a mismatched transducer is a false economy.

## FAQ

**Do I need side imaging on a fish finder under $300?**
Honestly, no — at this price side imaging usually means a small screen or a unit that ships with a basic transducer and needs an upgrade to use it. Down imaging is the more realistic upgrade under $300, and traditional CHIRP sonar plus GPS will find more fish for most anglers than a cramped side-imaging view.

**Is a 5-inch screen big enough for a fish finder?**
For a kayak or a small boat, yes. For a console unit where you're running split views and mapping, a 5-inch screen gets crowded fast, and most anglers who fish structure regularly end up wanting a 7-inch screen. If you can stretch to a 7-inch unit near $300, it's worth it.

**Do these units come with a transducer?**
Most of the units in this roundup ship with a transom-mount transducer in the box, but you should confirm before buying — some listings sell the head unit alone or bundle a different transducer. Check the model number carefully, because the same head unit is often sold in multiple bundles.

**Can I use a fish finder under $300 for ice fishing?**
Yes, several of these work for ice fishing, especially the Garmin Striker 4 and the Humminbird units. You'll typically need an ice transducer or a portable shuttle to run the unit off a small battery, and a flasher-style display mode helps, so check that the unit supports one before you commit.

**Do I need a mapping card, or is the built-in map enough?**
The built-in basemaps on these units are basic — they show coastline and major contours but not the fine structure you want for fishing. If you fish unfamiliar water, a LakeMaster or Navionics card is worth the extra money, and units like the Helix 5 and Elite FS 7 accept them. On a lake you know well, Quickdraw-style mapping or waypoints may be all you need.
