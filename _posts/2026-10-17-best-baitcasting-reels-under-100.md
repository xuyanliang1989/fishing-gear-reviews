## What to look for

- **Braking system quality.** A magnetic or dual-brake system that lets you dial in control matters more than bearing count. Look for externally adjustable brakes — you'll want to change settings as you switch between a heavy jig and a light Texas rig.
- **Drag smoothness and max drag.** For bass and inshore work you want at least 10–15 lbs of max drag, but smoothness matters more than the number. A gritty 15-lb drag loses fish; a smooth 11-lb drag doesn't.
- **Frame material and weight.** Graphite-composite frames keep the price down but flex under load. Aluminum frames are heavier but more rigid. Under $100 you're mostly looking at composite, so check reviews for frame flex complaints.
- **Gear ratio and line capacity.** A 6.2:1 to 7.3:1 ratio covers most bass and inshore applications. Make sure the spool holds enough 12-lb mono or 30-lb braid for the fishing you actually do.

## FAQ

**Do I need to spend $100 to get a decent baitcaster?**
No. The sub-$60 reels like the Black Max are genuinely usable for bass and panfish, especially if you're learning. The jump to the $80–$100 range mainly buys you a smoother drag, a more rigid frame, and better brake tunability — things that matter more as you fish harder and target bigger fish.

**What gear ratio should I get for my first baitcaster?**
A 6.2:1 to 7.3:1 ratio covers the widest range of techniques. Slower ratios (6.x:1) are better for cranking and working big baits, while faster ratios (7.x:1) are better for topwater and burning moving baits. If you only buy one reel, a 7.3:1 is the most versatile starting point.

**How often should I clean and oil a budget baitcaster?**
More often than you'd think — every 15–20 trips or at least once a season if you fish regularly. Budget reels use less expensive bearings, so a drop of reel oil on the spool bearings and a light cleaning of the drag washers goes a long way toward keeping things smooth. Saltwater use requires a rinse and re-oil after every trip.

**Can I use a baitcaster for saltwater inshore fishing?**
Yes, but rinse it with fresh water after every trip and re-oil the bearings, and expect a shorter lifespan than a dedicated saltwater reel. The Daiwa Fuego CT and Shimano SLX A handle light inshore work fine, but avoid dunking them and stay on top of maintenance.

**Why does my baitcaster backlash even with the brakes turned up?**
Usually it's spool tension, not the brakes. Set the spool tension so a lure falls slowly and the spool stops when the lure hits the ground, then use the brake dial to fine-tune for wind and lure weight. Also check that you're not overfilling the spool — a spool filled to the brim backlashes far more easily than one filled to about 1/8 inch from the rim.
