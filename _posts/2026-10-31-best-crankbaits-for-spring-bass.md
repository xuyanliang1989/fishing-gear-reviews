Spring crankbait fishing isn't about throwing a crankbait — it's about throwing the *right* crankbait for a prespawn-to-postspawn window that changes almost weekly. Pick wrong and you're dragging a bait through the strike zone at the wrong speed, at the wrong depth, with the wrong action. This guide covers the baits that actually earn their place in a spring box, from flat-sided balsa-style crawdad imitators to mid-depth squarebills, with real prices and honest trade-offs.

## What to look for

Spring bass behavior drives bait selection more than any marketing claim. Here's what actually matters:

- **Running depth matched to the prespawn migration.** Early spring fish suspend on points and channel swings; late spring fish push to flats. A bait that runs 4–8 feet covers most of that range. Deep divers (15+ feet) are usually a summer tool.
- **Tight, wide, or silent action.** Cold water favors tighter wobble and silent or low-rattle baits; once the water hits the mid-50s, a wider, noisier squarebill gets more reaction strikes.
- **Flat sides vs. round body.** Flat-sided baits deflect sharper and imitate crawdads and shad better in clear, cold water. Round baits hunt wider and shine in dirty water.
- **Hook quality and hardware.** Stock trebles on budget baits are the first thing to fail on a 5-pounder. Look for name-brand hooks or budget for replacements.

## FAQ

**What depth crankbait should I throw in early spring?**
Early prespawn, most bass are staging in that 4–10 foot range on points and channel swings, so a bait that runs 4–8 feet covers the bulk of it. Deep divers (15+ feet) are usually a summer tool unless you're fishing a steep, deep reservoir where fish hold deep longer.

**Should I use a rattling or silent crankbait in cold spring water?**
In water below about 50 degrees, a silent or low-rattle bait with a tight action usually outperforms a loud, wide-wobbling one. Once the water warms into the mid-50s and fish get more aggressive, rattling squarebills and wider actions start drawing more reaction strikes.

**Flat-sided or round-bodied crankbait for spring bass?**
Flat-sided baits deflect sharper and imitate crawdads better in clear, cold water, while round-bodied baits hunt wider and move more water in stained or dirty conditions. Match the body style to water clarity and how aggressive the fish are.

**What colors work best for spring crankbaits?**
Crawdad patterns (brown, orange, red) are the standard for prespawn bass feeding on the bottom, while shad and baitfish patterns work when fish are keyed on suspended forage. In stained water, brighter chartreuse and craw combos help fish find the bait.

**Do I need to upgrade the hooks on budget spring crankbaits?**
On most sub-ten-dollar baits, the stock trebles are the weak link, especially when you're banging them off rock and cover all day. Swapping to a quality replacement treble is cheap insurance against losing a good fish at the boat.
